Wills

Your will sets out who receives your assets, who will act as your executor, and who you would like to care for any children under 18. Without a valid will, your estate is distributed under a fixed legal formula that may not reflect your wishes.

We prepare wills for individuals and couples, and explain each part in plain language before you sign.

  • Gifts to family, friends and charities
  • Reviewing and updating an existing will

When to review your will

A will should change as your life does. It is worth reviewing yours after:

  • marriage, separation or divorce
  • the birth of a child or grandchild
  • buying or selling property or a business
  • the death of a beneficiary or executor

Testamentary trusts

A testamentary trust is created by your will and comes into effect after your death. It can help protect an inheritance for young or vulnerable beneficiaries, and may offer tax and asset protection benefits for your family.

Special disability trusts

A special disability trust allows you to provide for a family member with a severe disability, with care and accommodation needs met from the trust. We can explain how these trusts work and whether one suits your family.

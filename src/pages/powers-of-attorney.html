A will only takes effect after your death. Powers of attorney cover the time before, if you lose the ability to manage your own affairs. Without them, your family may need to apply to VCAT before they can act for you.

Enduring power of attorney

Appoints one or more people to make financial and/or personal decisions on your behalf. It continues if you lose capacity, which is when it is needed most.

General (non-enduring) power of attorney

Allows someone to act for you for a set purpose or period, for example while you are overseas. It ends if you lose capacity.

Medical treatment decision maker

Appoints a person to make medical treatment decisions for you if you are unable to make them yourself.

Advance care directive

Records your values and your instructions about future medical treatment, so your wishes are known and respected.

Choosing your attorney

Your attorney should be someone you trust completely and who is willing and able to take on the role. We explain the responsibilities involved and help you decide who to appoint, whether to appoint more than one person, and what powers to give.
